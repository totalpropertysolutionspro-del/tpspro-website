What Does Commercial Cleaning Cost in Albany, NY? The Cost Drivers Explained

Why there is no honest price list for commercial cleaning — and the factors that actually move the number on your quote.

By the TPS Pro team · May 29, 2026 · 15 min read

If you're a property manager, office manager, or business owner in Albany NY and you're trying to figure out what commercial cleaning should cost, the honest answer is: it varies — a lot. A 1,200 sq ft insurance office with two bathrooms has a completely different cleaning profile than a 12,000 sq ft student housing complex or a 4,000 sq ft medical practice. The scope, frequency, soil level, and type of space all drive the number.

That is why TPS Pro does not publish a price list for commercial cleaning in Albany — every job is walked and quoted on its own scope. What we can do is show you exactly what drives the number, so you can read any quote with confidence. If one bid comes in dramatically lower than the rest, that's usually a sign that something is missing — either coverage, labor, or the scope of what's actually included.

This guide breaks down the cost drivers for commercial cleaning in Albany NY by space type, explains what moves the price up or down, and walks through what's included in a standard recurring service versus what costs extra. If you're about to put out an RFP or call a few cleaning companies for quotes, this is what you need to know first.

What Factors Affect Commercial Cleaning Cost in Albany?

Before you compare quotes, it helps to understand the four main levers that determine what you'll actually pay. Most commercial cleaning quotes are built around all four of these variables.

Square Footage

Square footage is the starting point for any commercial cleaning quote. More space means more labor hours, and labor is the largest cost in commercial cleaning. A 5,000 sq ft office building takes roughly three times as long to clean as a 1,500 sq ft suite. That said, square footage is not the only factor — a 5,000 sq ft warehouse with concrete floors and minimal furniture cleans faster than a 5,000 sq ft dental practice with operatories, sterilization rooms, and a busy waiting area.

Cleaning Frequency

How often you want service has a major impact on per-visit cost. Daily service is more efficient for a cleaning crew — they have a routine, the space does not accumulate as much between visits, and scheduling is predictable. Weekly or biweekly service requires more time per visit because soil has built up, and one-time cleans require even more time because the baseline condition is unknown. As a general rule, the less frequently you clean, the higher the cost per visit — but the lower the total monthly cost.

Soil Level and Space Type

A law office stays relatively clean between visits. A restaurant or commercial kitchen gets hammered every service day. Student housing units, medical offices, and industrial facilities all carry soil loads that take more time, more product, and sometimes specialized equipment to address properly. Higher soil level means higher cost per hour for the same square footage.

Scope of Work

Standard recurring cleaning covers the basics: vacuuming, mopping, trash removal, restroom sanitization, surface wiping, and glass. Specialty services — carpet extraction, floor stripping and waxing, pressure washing, post-construction cleanup, deep appliance cleaning — are priced separately. One of the most common surprises for business owners is receiving a competitive recurring quote and then discovering that the floor care they assumed was included is actually a separate contract requiring additional budget.

Small Offices (Under 2,000 sq ft)

A typical small office in Albany — a two-room insurance agency, a solo accounting firm, a small professional suite in an office park — usually needs weekly cleaning. That covers vacuuming carpets, mopping hard floors, emptying trash, wiping down surfaces and desks, cleaning one or two restrooms, and addressing the kitchen or break area. If you add a second weekly visit, the second visit usually costs less than the first — not double — because the space is better maintained and each visit takes less time.

At the lighter end, you are typically looking at basic weekly service in a very tidy environment with minimal restroom usage. At the heavier end, you have more traffic, multiple restrooms, or a kitchen that sees daily use and accumulates grease and food residue.

Medium Offices (2,000–5,000 sq ft)

Medium-sized offices — an 8-person financial firm, a chiropractic practice, a regional nonprofit, a real estate brokerage — show the widest variation in labor. An open-plan office with one restroom is quick to clean; a multi-room suite with private offices, a conference room, multiple restrooms, and a full break room takes far longer for the same square footage.

At this size, most businesses also start thinking seriously about floor care as a separate line item. Carpets that get vacuumed weekly will still need hot water extraction every 6 to 12 months to remove embedded soil and extend carpet life. Hard floors that get mopped weekly will need stripping and refinishing once or twice a year depending on finish type and foot traffic. Budget these as separate, periodic add-ons — not part of your monthly recurring rate.

Large Commercial Spaces (5,000 sq ft and Up)

Large commercial facilities — office buildings, multi-tenant professional complexes, large retail locations, educational facilities — often need a multi-person crew on each visit, and the scope expands to include more restrooms, larger common areas, loading zones, and sometimes outdoor trash and entry maintenance.

Configuration matters as much as size here. A 10,000 sq ft warehouse that is mostly open floor space with two offices and two restrooms may clean in under two hours. A 10,000 sq ft medical facility with exam rooms, waiting areas, clinical spaces, and high sanitation requirements takes significantly longer and may require specialized cleaning protocols. Know what type of square footage you have before requesting quotes — the same building size can produce very different labor requirements.

Medical and Dental Offices

Medical and dental practices take more labor than a standard office of the same size. The reasons are concrete: healthcare environments call for EPA-registered disinfectants with appropriate dwell times, bloodborne pathogen awareness, careful handling around sharps disposal areas, more rigorous restroom and clinical surface sanitization, and often a documentation trail the practice wants to see. Crews working in medical environments need to follow the practice's own protocols.

That extra time is justified — cutting corners in a clinical environment creates real liability. If a cleaning company quotes a medical practice exactly like a generic office, ask specifically how they handle disinfection, how their staff are trained, and what products they use in clinical areas. The answers will tell you a great deal about whether they actually understand healthcare cleaning. Our medical office cleaning page explains how we scope these accounts.

Restaurants and Food Service

Restaurant and food service cleaning takes more time than office cleaning due to grease, food residue, high-traffic flooring, and the requirement for a consistently sanitary public environment. Front-of-house cleaning — dining room, bar top, restrooms, host stand, entry — is scoped much like a busy retail environment. Back-of-house kitchen cleaning is a separate category entirely and is usually handled nightly by kitchen staff or contracted separately with a company that specializes in hood cleaning and grease management.

Student Housing Turnovers (Per Unit)

Student housing turnover cleaning is quoted per unit, per turnover — not on a monthly recurring basis. The main drivers are unit size and condition at move-out. A studio or one-bedroom apartment in average condition is quick. A 4-bedroom unit in poor condition — heavy kitchen grease buildup, significant carpet staining, walls that need scrubbing throughout, debris left in closets — takes far longer, and may need repair, paint and patch work on top of the clean.

Volume is the other big lever. TPS Pro has turned 800+ units in a single turn and more than 5,000 units to date for Capital Region student-housing operators, and one crew handling cleaning, repairs, paint and common areas across a whole building is more efficient than a dozen separate vendors. Post-Construction Cleanup

Post-construction cleaning is quoted by project scope, not by recurring visit. The range of effort is wide: a light final clean after a cosmetic renovation in an otherwise finished space is at one end; a full three-phase rough, final, and detail clean on a ground-up commercial construction project is at the other. One-Time vs. Recurring Commercial Cleaning: Why Recurring Costs Less Per Visit

This is one of the most misunderstood dynamics in commercial cleaning pricing. Business owners sometimes compare a one-time quote to a recurring quote and are surprised that the per-visit rate is lower for recurring service. The reason is straightforward economics.

When a cleaning company commits a crew to a recurring account — say, every Tuesday night from 6 to 9 pm — they build that into their scheduling with certainty. They know the space, they have a routine, they bring exactly the right equipment every visit, and they are not spending time on a site assessment before each clean. The mobilization cost — driving to the site, loading the van, briefing the crew on a new location — is amortized across many visits. And because the space is cleaned regularly, each visit starts from a maintained baseline and takes less time than starting from scratch.

A one-time commercial clean in Albany will typically cost noticeably more per visit than the equivalent recurring service. This is normal and expected — it reflects the real cost structure of a non-recurring job, not opportunistic pricing. Any legitimate cleaning company should be able to explain this differential clearly.

The practical takeaway: if your business needs cleaning with any regularity at all, commit to a recurring schedule. Even biweekly service produces a meaningfully lower per-visit cost than ad-hoc one-time cleans. And you will get a more consistent result because the crew knows your space, your preferences, and your standards.

What Is Included in Standard Commercial Cleaning vs. What Costs Extra

One of the most common sources of surprise invoices in commercial cleaning is the assumption that everything is included when it is not. Here is a clear breakdown of what standard recurring commercial cleaning in Albany typically covers, and what is almost always priced as a separate service.

Typically Included in Recurring Service

  • Vacuuming all carpeted areas including edges and corners
  • Mopping all hard surface floors — vinyl, tile, wood, and concrete
  • Emptying and relining all trash and recycling receptacles
  • Full restroom sanitization: toilets, urinals, sinks, mirrors, counters, and replenishing paper products and soap where stocked
  • Wiping down horizontal surfaces: desks, counters, conference tables, and windowsills
  • Dusting accessible surfaces, low shelving, and exposed equipment
  • Interior glass and partition cleaning at accessible heights
  • Break room and kitchen cleaning: counters, sink, microwave exterior, and basic appliance exterior wipedown
  • Entry and lobby maintenance including mats and glass doors

Typically Extra (Specialty Services Priced Separately)

  • Carpet hot water extraction: Quoted by area; scheduled quarterly or semi-annually depending on traffic
  • Hard floor stripping and waxing or refinishing: Labor-intensive; quoted by area, finish type and condition
  • Deep appliance cleaning: Refrigerator interior, commercial oven cleaning, dishwasher deep clean — usually priced per appliance
  • High dusting: Ceiling fans, exposed ductwork, HVAC vents above 8 feet — requires ladders and extra crew time
  • Exterior window cleaning: Both ground-floor and multi-story; always a separate quote
  • Pressure washing: Exterior entries, loading docks, and parking areas
  • Post-construction cleanup: Always a separate project scope and pricing, never bundled into recurring service
  • Biohazard or hazardous material remediation: A separate specialty service requiring licensed contractors

When reviewing a commercial cleaning proposal, ask specifically: "What floor care is included in this contract, and what is priced separately?" This single question will head off most billing disputes before they start.

How to Get an Accurate Commercial Cleaning Quote in Albany

If you are calling cleaning companies for quotes, the more information you provide upfront, the more accurate the quote you will receive. Companies that give you a firm number without this information are either guessing or quoting low to win the contract and planning to revise later. Here is what to have ready before you make the call.

Information to Have Ready When Requesting a Quote

  • Total square footage of cleanable space, broken down by floor type if you know it (carpet vs. hard surface)
  • Space type — office, medical, retail, industrial, student housing, restaurant — be specific about what happens in the space
  • Number of restrooms and approximate daily usage volume
  • Desired frequency: Daily, three times per week, weekly, biweekly, or one-time
  • Service window preferences: Daytime, after-hours, or weekend — does your space allow evening or weekend access for cleaning crews?
  • Current condition: New space never professionally cleaned, replacing an existing vendor, or first-time professional service
  • Special requirements: Healthcare sanitation protocols, green or eco-certified products, specific product restrictions, security clearance or key card requirements for crew access
  • Add-ons needed: Floor care, exterior window cleaning, trash hauling, or periodic deep cleans

A reputable cleaning company will want to do a walkthrough before finalizing any quote. For anything over 2,000 sq ft, a site visit is standard practice. Be cautious of companies that will give you a firm monthly number via email or phone without ever seeing the space — they will almost certainly revise the price after starting work, and virtually always upward. A surprise price increase after the first month is a leading indicator of a company that was not honest in the proposal process.

Red Flags When Hiring a Commercial Cleaning Company in Albany

Commercial cleaning is an industry with relatively low barriers to entry, which means there is a wide range of quality and reliability among providers. Choosing the wrong vendor does not just mean a dirty office — it means liability exposure, inconsistent service, and the hassle of finding a replacement mid-contract. Here is what to watch for before you sign anything.

The Price Is Too Low

If one quote is far below every other bid you received, there is a reason for that gap. The most common explanations: the company is quoting a scope that does not match what you asked for, they are planning to use fewer labor hours than the job actually requires and hoping you do not notice the shortcuts, they are not carrying proper insurance and workers' compensation, or they are a very new company trying to land accounts at any price. Any of these creates problems that will cost you more in the long run.

Legitimate commercial cleaning companies in Albany operate with real labor costs. New York's minimum wage keeps rising, and experienced cleaning staff earn more than minimum in a competitive labor market. Add payroll taxes, employer-side workers' compensation, general liability insurance, equipment, vehicle costs, supplies, and overhead, and you will understand quickly why a weekly 3-hour clean of a 3,000 sq ft office has a real floor under it. If a number looks too good to be true, something is wrong with the math.

No Proof of Insurance

Always ask for a certificate of insurance showing general liability coverage at the limits your lease or management agreement requires, plus workers' compensation coverage for all employees, before any cleaning company enters your building. Request that your business be named as an additional insured on the policy. A legitimate company will provide this documentation without hesitation or delay. If a company is reluctant or cannot produce workers' comp documentation, they are likely using 1099 independent contractors — which can create co-employment liability for your business if someone is injured on your property.

No Local References

Ask for two or three references from current commercial clients in the Albany or Capital Region area — not residential cleaning clients, and not accounts from several years ago. A company that cannot provide current local business references is either new to the market or cycling through clients at a rate that prevents them from accumulating long-term relationships. Call the references and ask specifically about reliability over time, how the company handles missed items or complaints, and whether they would rehire the company. Any cleaning company can deliver a strong first visit. The question is whether they still show up consistently six months or two years into the contract.

No Written Scope of Work

A verbal agreement about what is included is worth nothing when a dispute arises over a missed restroom or a floor that never gets mopped. Before any cleaning company begins work in your building, you need a written service agreement that specifies: exactly what tasks are performed on each visit, what tasks are not included in the scope, the service frequency, the pricing, and the process for requesting additional services or raising quality concerns. If a company will not put the scope in writing, walk away regardless of how competitive the price looks.

No Quality Control Process

How does the company verify that the work was completed correctly? Do crew leads use standardized checklists? Do supervisors conduct periodic inspections? Is there a reporting mechanism for missed tasks or quality issues? Companies vary significantly in their approach to quality control. A company that has no answer to these questions is relying entirely on the crew showing up and doing the right thing without any verification system — which functions adequately until it does not. Ask specifically how problems are documented and resolved, and what happens if you call with a complaint after a service visit.
