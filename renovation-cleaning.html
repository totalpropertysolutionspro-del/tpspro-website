Who it's for

Renovations where people are still in the building

Post-construction cleaning assumes an empty site. Most Capital Region renovations aren't empty.

A law firm redoing one floor while the rest of the office works. A medical practice renovating exam rooms one at a time. An apartment building updating units while neighbors live next door. A store remodeling a department while the registers stay open. In every one of these, the problem isn't just cleaning the finished work — it's keeping dust and debris from spreading through the parts of the building people are using.

That's what renovation cleaning is for. We clean alongside the project, not just at the end. Your contractor builds; we keep the building usable around them and hand each finished area back ready for occupants. If you're running a new build or an empty fit-out, you want our post-construction cleaning instead.

Dust control

Keeping the dust where the work is

  • Coordinate with the contractor's plastic containment and zip doors
  • Walk-off mats at every zone exit, changed as they load up
  • Nightly damp-wipe of surfaces next to the work zone
  • Vacuum before mopping so fine dust is picked up, not smeared
  • Clean the work path: corridors, elevators, stairwells, restrooms
  • Wipe vents and returns near the work area; flag HVAC concerns to the contractor

Fine drywall and sanding dust is the main complaint during an occupied renovation. It rides on shoes and carts, drifts through doorways and settles on desks two rooms away. Dry dusting just lifts it back into the air.

We use damp methods, clean from high to low, and focus on the routes people and materials take through the building. The contractor owns the containment; we make sure what escapes it doesn't become your staff's or tenants' problem.

Phased cleans

How a renovation clean runs

Plan the zones

We walk the project with you and the contractor, map the work zones, occupied areas and routes, and agree when we can be in each space.

Clean during the work

Nightly or between trade phases, we clean the work path and adjacent occupied areas and clear light debris so the building stays usable.

Final clean per zone

When a zone finishes, it gets a full detail clean — dust top to bottom, glass, fixtures, vents, floors — and is handed back for use.

Touch-up at close-out

At the end of the project we sweep the whole building once more for settled dust and last-trade marks.

FAQ

Renovation cleaning questions, answered

How is renovation cleaning different from post-construction cleaning?

Post-construction cleaning is the handoff clean for a finished, usually empty, build. Renovation cleaning happens while the building is still in use: we keep dust out of occupied areas during the work, clean in phases as each area finishes, and return each zone to service. See post-construction cleaning for new builds and full fit-outs.

Can you clean while renovation work is still going on?

Yes. That's the core of the service. We clean the work path and adjacent occupied areas nightly or between trade phases, coordinate with your contractor, and stay out of active work zones.

How do you control dust in occupied areas?

We work with your contractor's containment, keep walk-off mats at zone exits, damp-wipe surfaces instead of dry-dusting, vacuum before mopping, and clean corridors, elevators and restrooms along the work path so dust doesn't travel through the building.

Do you handle both residential and commercial renovation projects?

Yes. We clean occupied office floors, medical and retail spaces under renovation, apartment buildings with units being renovated while neighbors live next door, and individual units and homes after a remodel.

Can you clean one floor or wing at a time?

Yes. Phased renovations are common. Each zone gets its own final clean when the trades finish it, so you can reopen it to staff, tenants or customers while work moves to the next area.

Do you offer renovation cleaning in Saratoga Springs?

Yes. We serve Saratoga Springs and all of Saratoga County, plus Albany, Troy, Schenectady, Clifton Park, Cohoes, Colonie, Latham, Guilderland and the rest of the Capital Region.
