# TPS Pro: Market, Competitor & SEO Research for the Site Overhaul

Prepared 2026-09-27 for totalpropertysolution.net (Total Property Solutions Pro LLC, Albany NY).
Method: live Google-style SERP checks (WebSearch), competitor page fetches (WebFetch), and an audit of the local repo at `/Users/miguelmedina/Desktop/tpspro-website`. No keyword-tool volume data was available (the Ahrefs/Similarweb connectors are not authorized), so priorities come from what the SERPs look like: who ranks, whether directories and aggregators take the top spots, and whether the results are local or national. Treat every "priority" below as a judgment call, not a measured number.

## 3. What makes B2B service sites convert in this niche

Evidence plus what the competitors show:

1. **Put risk-reduction proof on the homepage, not three clicks deep.** Facility managers "are screening for risk, reliability, and whether your process makes scope clear before pricing." Bonding and insurance, OSHA compliance and credentials belong on the homepage, with **specific coverage amounts** and certificate details, not just the words "fully insured." (https://www.abstraktmg.com/build-a-website-for-commercial-cleaning-business/ ; https://squarerootseo.com/blog/website-trust-signals-that-convert/)
   - TPS action: add a trust bar directly under the hero: "Bonded & insured: $X GL / $X umbrella · Workers' comp · COI in 1 business day · Since 2019 · 5,000+ units turned · 4.6★ Google (30)". Link it to `/vendor-onboarding`.
2. **Case studies with numbers.** The Turn Company and Cleantec lead with numbers (beds, retention %, staff). The student-housing buyer's main fear is missed move-in dates and too little crew capacity. Operators "confirm primary and backup crews... deadlines, backup capacity, and a clear escalation path" (https://hhredstone.com/student-housing-turnover-planning/). Turn season means one operator turning a huge number of beds in a two-week window (https://rapideyeinspections.com/research/student-housing-turn-season-statistics/).
   - TPS action: publish 3 case studies in the format *Client type → Challenge → Scope → Crew size and schedule → Result (units per day, % on time, punch items closed) → quote*. Start with the "800-unit single turn," a post-construction project, and a recurring commercial account. Use client names only with permission; otherwise say "national student-housing operator, 800 beds, Albany."
3. **Capability statement (one-page PDF).** This is standard for government, university and GC procurement. Include NAICS codes, UEI/SAM status, certifications, core competencies, past performance and contact details, on one page and one side. (https://iquasar.com/blog/guide-to-writing-a-capability-statement-for-government-contractors/ ; https://govscout.io/capability-statement/ ; https://www.sba.gov/document/brand-guide-8a-capabilities-statement)
4. **Vendor onboarding with no friction:** a W-9 download, COI request form, insurance summary and supported vendor portals (see Cluster H). Property managers at PeakMade, Greystar-type operators and Beacon usually onboard vendors through compliance platforms, so saying "already credentialed / ready to credential" removes a delay.
5. **Response-time guarantees, stated specifically.** Daigle's "100% satisfaction guarantee with 24-hour remedy" and Cleantec's "24/7 emergency" are the local benchmarks. Speed-to-lead research: responding within 5 minutes makes a lead ~21× more likely to qualify than waiting 30 minutes (InsideSales/MIT). The average B2B response is ~42–47 hours. (https://www.insidesales.com/response-time-matters/ ; https://www.chilipiper.com/article/speed-to-lead-statistics)
   - TPS action: promise "Quote within 24 hours · walkthrough within 48 hours · emergencies answered 24/7 · re-clean within 24 hours, free." Wire forms to an instant SMS/email alert. The current mailto-based form handling is a conversion leak: many desktop users have no mail client set up. Deploy the Cloudflare Worker endpoint.
6. **An "Industries We Serve" section and pages.** JAN-PRO (17), Clean Method (25+) and Cleantec (6) all have one. Buyers look for their own vertical.
7. **Client logos.** The site already has a wordmark wall. Upgrade to real logos once you have permission, and add a "Trusted by" row to every money page, not just the homepage. The strongest names for B2B credibility are PeakMade, Beacon Communities, Rosenblum, Golub, AECOM and Cushman & Wakefield. Confirm permission for each before featuring them (the memory notes say AECOM, C&W and Golub are still pending Miguel's confirmation).
8. **Embedded Google reviews** (the actual review text, with author and date) near forms. Review content also feeds AI Overviews for local queries (§5). Ask each turnover client for a Google review right after the season ends. The goal is 60+ reviews at 4.7★ by next turn season.
9. **Before/after photo sliders and a crew photo in branded shirts.** Real site photos signal scale; generic stock photos signal a residential maid service (abstraktmg source above).
10. **Productized scopes.** The Turn Company's "14-item Make-Ready Punch" and System4's "seasonal cleaning plan" turn a vague service into something buyers can compare. TPS action: publish a "TPS Turn Standard" checklist (clean, patch, paint, repair, common areas, QC photos per unit) as HTML plus PDF.
11. **B2B-specific forms.** Add fields for company, property or portfolio name, unit or square-foot count, service needed, target date, and "I need: quote / walkthrough / vendor packet." Keep the form short (5–6 fields) with a progressive second step. Martinez's "Schedule a Walkthrough" CTA fits B2B better than "Free Estimate."
12. **Certifications worth pursuing,** in order of return:
    - **NYS MWBE (MBE) certification, if eligible.** State agencies have participation goals averaging ~30% on contracts over $25k (https://government.biz/state-local/new-york-contracts ; https://ogs.ny.gov/MWBE). Martinez and Summit both show MBE badges.
    - OSHA-10 for crew leads.
    - ISSA CIMS / CIMS-GB for janitorial credibility (Daigle has it). It's a longer path.

## 5. Technical and SEO standards to apply sitewide

### Titles and meta descriptions
- **Title:** 50–60 characters (Google shows ~580px on desktop, ~480px on mobile). Keyword first, brand last. (https://sitegrade.io/en/quick-answers/meta-description-title-tag-correct-lengths-2026/ ; https://mrs.digital/tools/meta-length-checker/)
  - Pattern: `[Service] in [City], NY | TPS Pro`, or for the flagship: `Student Housing Turnovers Albany NY – 800+ Units/Turn | TPS Pro`.
  - Several current titles are 35–45 characters, which leaves room to use. The JARVIS bot's band (30–65) is fine, but aim for 50–60.
- **Meta description:** 120–155 characters. Include a proof number and a CTA ("5,000+ units turned. Bonded & insured. Quote in 24 hrs.").
- **One H1 per page** that matches the page's main search. Several H1s currently read as fragments ("The one-stop shop for", "One company. One contract."), probably because a styled `<span>` splits the text. Make sure the full H1 text includes the keyword and city (e.g. "Student Housing Turnovers in Albany & the Capital Region").

### Schema (JSON-LD), 2026 practice
- **Organization** (sitewide, homepage): name, legalName "Total Property Solutions Pro LLC," url, logo, `sameAs` (GBP/Maps CID URL, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Yelp, BBB, Chamber listing), foundingDate 2019, contactPoint.
- **LocalBusiness** subtype `HomeAndConstructionBusiness` or `ProfessionalService` (schema.org has no CommercialCleaning type; `HousePainter` and `GeneralContractor` exist for the construction line). Since TPS is a service-area business, use `areaServed` (City/AdministrativeArea list) rather than a public street address, unless a staffed office is shown on GBP. Include telephone, openingHours (24/7 only if true), `aggregateRating` **only when the reviews are shown on that page** (self-serving review markup on a LocalBusiness page won't produce stars and may be ignored), `priceRange`, and `hasOfferCatalog`.
- **Service** on each service page: `serviceType`, `provider` (@id reference to the Organization), `areaServed`, `audience` (BusinessAudience: property managers, etc.).
- **BreadcrumbList** on every non-home page (currently only 16+8 pages have it).
- **Article/BlogPosting** with a real **Person author** (Miguel Medina, Owner) plus `datePublished`/`dateModified`.
- **FAQPage:** keep it on pages with visible FAQs. It no longer produces rich results (deprecated May 2026), but Google says it still uses the markup to understand pages, and AI engines extract Q&A well. (https://www.getpassionfruit.com/blog/what-changed-with-google-drops-faq-rich-results-and-what-to-do-now ; https://www.thehoth.com/blog/google-faq-rich-results-deprecated/)
- Tie everything together with one `@id` graph, e.g. `https://totalpropertysolution.net/#org`, referenced from every page.

### AI-search (answer-engine) visibility
- **Answer first:** open each money page and blog post with a 40–60-word factual summary: who, what, where, the proof number, and how to buy. For example: "TPS Pro is an Albany, NY turnover contractor that cleans, repairs and paints student and multifamily units, and has turned 800+ units in a single turn and 5,000+ since 2019..." (https://www.thehoth.com/blog/how-to-get-cited-in-ai-overviews/)
- **Third-party mentions carry the most weight:** brands are ~6.5× more likely to be cited through third-party sources than through their own sites. Directories, association listings, "best of" lists and local press (§4) are AI-visibility work. (https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/16/google-ai-overviews-chatgpt-perplexity-small-business-visibility-guide)
- **Bing indexing:** ChatGPT and Copilot use Bing, so verify the site in **Bing Webmaster Tools**, submit the sitemap, and enable **IndexNow** (easy on GitHub Pages via a key file plus pings on deploy).
- **robots.txt:** allow `OAI-SearchBot`, `ChatGPT-User`, `PerplexityBot`, `Google-Extended` (a choice; allowing it helps Gemini grounding), `ClaudeBot`/`Claude-SearchBot`, and `Bingbot`. Consider an `llms.txt` summary file. It's cheap, though its effect is unproven.
- **Consistent facts everywhere:** 800+ units per turn, 5,000+ units, since 2019, service area, phone (518) 948-7156. Resolve the GBP opening-date conflict (2011 vs 2019).
- **Comparison and pricing content:** pages like "Student turnover cost per unit in Albany" and "In-house maintenance crew vs. turnover contractor" match the questions buyers type into ChatGPT.

### Other technical items
- Set **noindex** on paid-ad landing pages (`*-lp.html`, `quote.html` optional, `thank-you.html`) so they don't compete with the organic money pages.
- **Internal links:** every blog post links to its owner money page. City pages link to the flagship and to the 3–4 services most relevant locally. Add a footer "Industries" column and a "For Procurement" link.
- **Core Web Vitals:** static GitHub Pages is a good base. Keep hero images under ~200KB (WebP/AVIF), preload the LCP image, and add width/height to avoid layout shift.
- **Forms:** replace mailto delivery with the Worker/Resend POST endpoint. Track `generate_lead` in GA4 and log phone clicks as conversions.
- **Sitemap:** include `lastmod`, exclude noindexed pages, and submit to both GSC and Bing.
- **Google Search Console:** connect it (still blocked, per the memory notes). Without it, you can't see which queries actually bring traffic, and this report's priorities stay estimates.
